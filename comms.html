NOAA Weather Radio
The one broadcast that keeps going when everything else is down. A weather radio with SAME alerting is the only thing on this page that wakes you up at 3 AM, and it needs no license, no app and no internet.
For the Wimberley Valley: WXK27 on 162.400 MHz. The Austin transmitter, 1,000 watts, licensed to cover Bastrop, Blanco, Burnet, Caldwell, Hays, Lee, Travis and Williamson counties. Program SAME code 048209 so a desk radio alarms only for Hays County.

SAME alerting

A dedicated weather radio (Midland and similar desktop models) or a handheld scanner with a weather alert function can sit silent until the National Weather Service issues a warning for your county, then sound an alarm. Set the county code to 048209 and it ignores warnings for surrounding counties.

Keep it plugged in with fresh backup batteries. This is the device that works when the cell network and the power are both out.

Warn Central Texas

The other thing that wakes you up. warncentraltexas.org is the free regional alert system run by CAPCOG for Hays and nine surrounding counties. Register a cell number and email for your street address and local emergency officials can reach you by text, call or email with evacuation and shelter notices for your neighborhood.

It depends on the cell network, so it complements a weather radio rather than replacing one.

MeshCore Mesh
Text messaging that works with no cell service, no internet and no grid power. No license, about $50, and it reaches the Valley.

What it is. Small solar-powered radios on hilltops and rooftops relay short text messages from one to the next across the region, using LoRa radio in the 900 MHz band. You carry a companion radio the size of a car key fob, paired to your phone over Bluetooth, and type in an app. Austin Mesh, a volunteer group, has built this network out from downtown Austin across the Hill Country, and as of mid-2026 it reaches Dripping Springs, Driftwood, Wimberley and San Marcos. Here in the Valley

The Wimberley Valley Amateur Radio Club hosts a solar repeater on a hilltop tower near the Square. It links directly to a downtown Austin node, with Driftwood, Fischer and the Dripping Springs cluster around it. Coverage in the Valley is good but not everywhere, and growing.

Nobody in the Valley runs an observer node yet. The nearest listening posts are around Dripping Springs. A resident who hosts one would put Wimberley on the live map.

WVARC members are active on the mesh. There is no club channel yet; the way in is Friday breakfast or Tuesday coffee (see Ham Repeaters).

Getting on

Radio: the Seeed Wio Tracker L1 Pro for MeshCore, about $50, comes pre-flashed in an enclosure with a battery and antenna. Nothing to install on the radio. An aftermarket antenna helps at the edge of coverage; RF Index publishes tested comparisons of radios and antennas.

App: MeshCore for iPhone or Android. Pair over Bluetooth, pick a handle, send an advert. Austin Mesh's setup walkthrough covers the rest.

Do it before you need it. A radio set up during a flood is a radio that doesn't work.

Two commands worth knowing

Join the #bot channel and send !flood for river gauge status or !nws for active weather warnings. A volunteer gateway answers over the radio. It also pushes flash flood and severe storm warnings to #alerts as they are issued.

Join the #meshwx channel and send wx 78676 for current conditions, warn 78676 for NWS warnings, or forecast 78676 for the forecast. Send help for the full list. A volunteer bot named WX-AUS answers on the channel. It gets its weather from a satellite dish, not the internet, so it keeps working when the internet is out. Replies are limited by distance, so if nothing comes back, you may be too many hops from the bot.

See it live

CoreScope shows every node and message the volunteer observers hear, on a map, in real time. Useful situational awareness even without a radio.

FRS, GMRS and MURS
Family and neighborhood radios. FRS is what most households already own; GMRS adds range and a Wimberley repeater for a $35 license with no exam.

FRS

The walkie-talkies sold in pairs at any big-box store. No license, up to 2 watts, channels 1 to 22. They share those channels with GMRS, so a family on FRS can talk to a neighbor on GMRS. Range is a mile or two in the Valley's terrain, less in the river bottoms.

GMRS

Same channels, more power, and repeaters. A $35 FCC license covers your whole household for ten years, no exam. Channels 1 to 7 allow up to 5 watts, 8 to 14 are limited to 0.5 watts, and 15 to 22 allow up to 50 watts from a mobile or base radio.

Channel 20, 462.675 with tone 141.3, is the informal travel and emergency channel. Not official, but widely monitored.

Public Safety, Hays County
Hays County law enforcement and fire/EMS now run mostly on LCRA's P25 trunked radio system, which a Baofeng or a basic scanner cannot receive. What's listed below is the conventional VHF that still carries audio.
Receive only. These are licensed public safety frequencies. Transmitting on them without authorization is a federal crime. Listen for situational awareness; call 911 to reach them.
